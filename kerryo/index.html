The only habit that matters

End every session with /save

/save

That's it. Everything else is optional sugar — this one keeps tomorrow's session from starting blind.

⌕
When you read something good

I want to keep this article / paper / thread.

ingest <url or file>

It pulls out the people, the ideas, and the claims — makes a page for each, cross-links them, and files them. One source becomes a dozen connected notes instead of a dead PDF in Downloads.

Why the inbox? New pages land in your inbox folder on purpose — the system won't guess whether a name is a work contact or a friend. You sort it when you triage.

When you've hoarded a pile

I saved ten things this week and read none of them.

ingest all of these

Parallel readers digest the whole batch, then cross-reference them against each other so the connections show up. Don't feed them one at a time — this is the weekend move.

When you're trying to remember

Wait — what do I actually know about this?

what do you know about <anything>?

It checks the recent cache, scans the index, drills into the right pages, and answers with links to the exact notes. Ask your own brain before you ask a search engine. If the answer is good, say "save that" and it becomes a note too.

When a session went well

That was a good conversation. Don't lose it.

/save

Turns the conversation into a clean, filed, linked note before you close the laptop. Yes — this is the habit from the top. It's here again because it's the whole game.

When it actually matters

I need to think this through, not blurt an answer.

/think <your hard question>

Runs a multi-stage reasoning loop over the problem instead of taking the first plausible route. Save it for the decisions you'll still care about next month.

When you want it researched

Go find out about this and don't make me watch.

/autoresearch <topic>

Autonomous rounds of search → fetch → synthesize → file. Come back to a set of fresh, sourced pages already sitting in the vault.

When the graph feels messy

Something's tangled in here.

lint the wiki

Finds orphan pages and dead links — and the good part, contradictions: when two notes disagree, it flags both, so your brain tells you when it disagrees with itself.

No play matches that. Try “ingest”, “save”, or “research”.
The weekly rhythm

Ten minutes, once a week

The whole maintenance routine. Do it on a weekend and the vault keeps itself honest.

1

ingest all of these — clear the week's saved pile out of your inbox.

2

Drag the new notes out of the inbox into wherever they belong.

3

lint the wiki — catch orphans, dead links, and contradictions.

Don't forget the daily one

End each working session with /save. The weekly rhythm keeps the vault tidy; /save keeps its memory alive.

How the memory works

So you trust it

No black box. Here's exactly what happens at the edges of a session.

🌅

Session start

It reads a recent-context cache first, so it opens already knowing what you're working on. No re-explaining.

🌙

Session end

The cache's date and "recently touched" list refresh on their own; the story part updates when you /save.

📄

Plain Markdown

All of it is plain Markdown on your disk. No database, no rented lock-in. If the tools vanished tomorrow, you'd still own every byte.

Why bother

Most note apps are graveyards — you dump things in and never find them again. This one does the linking and the finding for you, so the pile stops being dead weight and starts compounding like interest.

Day one it's a handful of pages. Month two, you ask your own vault first.
